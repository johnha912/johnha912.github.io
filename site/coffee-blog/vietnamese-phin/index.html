Vietnamese Phin

What is Vietnamese phin coffee?

Vietnamese phin coffee is a traditional brewing method using a phin: a small metal filter made of a chamber, a perforated plate, a press disc, and a lid. It produces a slow drip and a strong, rich coffee, often made from robusta beans, which have a more intense flavor and more caffeine than arabica. It’s typically served with sweetened condensed milk for a sweet, creamy drink, hot (cà phê nóng) or iced (cà phê đá / cà phê sữa đá), a favorite in Vietnam’s warm climate.

The phin’s simplicity and sustainability (no paper filters needed) make it an eco-friendly choice that fits modern brewing trends. Often described as a cross between a pour-over and a French press, it makes a concentrated brew you can enjoy with milk, with sugar, or black.

History and cultural significance

Coffee arrived in Vietnam in the mid-19th century during French colonization. A French priest brought an arabica tree in 1857, and over time Vietnam became a major coffee producer, known especially for robusta (Nguyen, 2022). The phin likely took hold in rural areas, where condensed milk was used for sweetening thanks to its long shelf life, a sign of local resourcefulness (Copper Cow Coffee, n.d.). The method has become a beloved tradition, symbolizing Vietnam’s coffee culture and its economic transformation through coffee exports.

How to make it at home

You need very little equipment. Here’s the step-by-step guide by Nguyen Coffee Supply:

  1. Set up. Place the phin filter plate and brew chamber on top of a glass.Make sure it’s stable for even dripping.
  2. Add coffee. Add 2 tablespoons (about 14 g) of Vietnamese coffee per serving.Use a dark roast with a fine grind, like sand.
  3. Level and press. Shake to even out the coffee, then drop the gravity press on top.This ensures even extraction.
  4. Bloom. Add 1 oz of water (about ½ inch above the press) and bloom for 45 seconds.Blooming releases gases and improves flavor.
  5. Fill. Add water to the top of the filter. Do a second pour for a less strong coffee.The second pour dilutes the intensity.
  6. Watch the first drip. The first drip should fall before 2 minutes.This tells you the grind size and water temperature are right.
  7. Finish. The last drop should fall around 5 minutes.Total brew time: about 5 minutes, prep included.
  8. Ice or hot. Add ice, or enjoy it hot.For iced, prepare with ice; for hot, serve immediately.
  9. Enjoy. With sweetened condensed milk, sugar, milk, or black.Condensed milk is traditional; customize to taste.

Variations are welcome: adjust the coffee-to-water ratio, or use honey instead of sugar. For traditional flavor, choose a dark roast, preferably robusta. Brands like Copper Cow Coffee offer ready-to-brew options (Copper Cow Coffee, n.d.), though grinding your own beans gives the full experience.

Vietnamese phin coffee is more than a drink. It’s a bridge to understanding Vietnam’s coffee culture, its historical resilience, and its modern economic impact. Try it at home, and you may discover an unexpected appreciation for a beverage rooted in colonial history and local innovation.
