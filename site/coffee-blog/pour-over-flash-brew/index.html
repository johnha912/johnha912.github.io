Pour Over / Flash Brew

What is Pour-Over?

A pour-over is a manual coffee brewing method where hot water is poured over ground coffee in a filter, letting gravity pull the brewed coffee down into a vessel below. It puts you in control of water temperature, pouring speed, and bloom time, so you can extract a well-balanced cup with nuanced flavors and aromas. The technique is favored for highlighting a coffee’s unique character, especially with single-origin beans (Scott, 2019).

Steps

  1. Prepare and preheat. Place the filter in the dripper and rinse it with hot water to remove any paper taste and preheat the dripper and carafe. Discard the rinse water.
  2. Add coffee grounds. Add 15–20 g of freshly ground coffee to the dripper and gently shake to level the bed for even extraction.
  3. Bloom. Start the timer and pour 40 g of water, enough to saturate the grounds. Let the coffee bloom (release trapped gases) for 30–45 seconds. This step improves flavor clarity.
  4. Main pour. Starting from the center, pour the remaining water slowly in a steady spiral until you reach 300 g, covering all the grounds evenly. Aim to finish pouring within 1.5–2 minutes.
  5. Drawdown. Let the water draw down fully through the grounds, usually finishing around 3–3.5 minutes, bloom and pour included.
  6. Serve and enjoy. Remove the dripper, give the coffee a gentle swirl, and pour it into your cup.

Flash Brew

Flash brew, also known as Japanese iced coffee, brews hot coffee directly over ice. You use a pour-over setup where part of the water is replaced by ice in the carafe. The hot coffee cools almost instantly, preserving its aromatic compounds and acidity for a bright, flavorful iced coffee. It lets a coffee’s complexity shine in a cold format, which makes it a favorite for high-quality single-origin beans (McCarthy, 2018; Rao, 2019).

Steps

  1. Prepare ice. Place 140 g of ice in the carafe under your pour-over dripper.
  2. Bloom. Pour about 40 g of hot water over the grounds to saturate them and let them bloom for 30–45 seconds. This releases carbon dioxide for a cleaner extraction.
  3. First pour. After the bloom, pour slowly in a circular motion until you reach 100 g, letting the water flow through evenly.
  4. Second pour. Continue to 160 g, finishing the pour in about 1.5–2 minutes. Total brew time, bloom included, should be around 2.5–3 minutes.
  5. Chill and swirl. The hot coffee melts part of the ice, cooling quickly and keeping its aroma and acidity. Swirl the carafe gently so it cools evenly.

Tips

Grind size: for flash brew, aim for a grind similar to table salt, then adjust for your dripper and preferred strength.

Ice-to-water ratio: more ice gives a lighter brew; less ice gives a stronger cup.
